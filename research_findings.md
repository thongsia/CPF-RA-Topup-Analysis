## 2. CPF Cash Top-up Relief

### Cap for Self Top-ups
- **Maximum tax relief for self top-ups: $8,000 per YA** (from YA 2023 onwards)
- Maximum for family members: $8,000 per YA
- Combined maximum (self + family): $16,000 per YA
- Cash top-ups that attract MRSS matching grants are **NOT eligible** for tax relief from YA 2026 (top-ups received from 1 Jan 2025)
- Cash top-ups to MediSave attracting MMSS grants will **NOT qualify** from YA 2027 (top-ups from 1 Jan 2026)

### Personal Income Tax Relief Cap
- **Total personal tax relief cap: $80,000 per YA** (applies to ALL reliefs combined, including CPF cash top-up relief)

### How It Works
- Relief is granted automatically — no claim needed
- Cash top-ups made in calendar year 2025 → relief in YA 2026
- For recipients aged 55+, top-up is capped at current year's FRS for tax relief purposes (though actual top-up can go up to ERS)

### Key Limit: Tax relief is capped at FRS, not ERS
- Tax relief for cash top-ups to RA (age 55+) is only given for the portion that fills up to the **Full Retirement Sum (FRS)**
- Top-ups above FRS (up to ERS) earn interest but generate **NO tax relief**
- FRS for YA 2026: $213,000; FRS for YA 2027: $220,400

**Source**: https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/tax-reliefs-rebates-and-deductions/tax-reliefs/central-provident-fund-(cpf)-cash-top-up-relief

## 3. Can Unused CPF Cash Top-up Relief Be Carried Forward?

**NO — personal tax reliefs CANNOT be carried forward to subsequent years.**

- Singapore personal tax reliefs (including CPF Cash Top-up Relief, Qualified Child Relief, etc.) are **use-it-or-lose-it** within the relevant Year of Assessment.
- Only **specific named rebates** (not reliefs) can be carried forward:
  - **Parenthood Tax Rebate (PTR)**: explicitly stated as carry-forward eligible
  - **Personal Income Tax Rebate** (YA 2024–2025 one-off): explicitly stated as NOT carry-forward eligible
- The IRAS CPF Cash Top-up Relief page makes **no mention** of carry-forward, confirming it is not available.
- Corporate tax has carry-forward rules for unutilised capital allowances and trade losses, but these do NOT apply to individual personal tax reliefs.

## 4. CPF Retirement Account (RA) Cap

### ⚠️ Clarification: The "$300,000" figure is outdated

The user's reference to a $300,000 lifetime cap for the RA is **not the current cap**. The relevant caps are:

### Current Retirement Sums (for members turning 55 in 2026)

| Sum | Amount |
|---|---|
| Basic Retirement Sum (BRS) | $110,200 |
| Full Retirement Sum (FRS) | $220,400 |
| **Enhanced Retirement Sum (ERS)** | **$440,800** |

- The **ERS is the maximum amount** a member aged 55+ can top up their RA to
- ERS increases every January (was $426,000 in 2025; now $440,800 in 2026)
- From 1 Jan 2025, the ERS was raised from **3× BRS to 4× BRS**
- Before the 2025 change, the ERS was ~$308,700–$319,500 (closer to the $300K figure)

### Historical ERS context
- Pre-2025 ERS: 3× BRS (~$308,700 for 2024)
- 2025 ERS: $426,000 (4× BRS of $106,500)
- **2026 ERS (current): $440,800** (4× BRS of $110,200)

### RA interest rate
- RA earns up to **4% base interest per annum** (government-guaranteed floor)
- Extra 1% on first $60,000 of combined CPF balances
- For members 55+: extra 2% on first $30,000 of combined balances, and extra 1% on next $30,000

### Top-up limit for RA
- Maximum top-up = ERS − RA savings (excluding interest earned, government grants, plus retirement withdrawals)
- Top-ups above FRS earn interest but do NOT qualify for tax relief
