# CPF RA Cash Top-Up: Option 1 vs Option 2 Comparison

*Scenario: existing RA balance $220,400 (FRS $220,400, headroom to FRS $0)*

## 1. Short Answer

**Option 1 (the $80,000 lump sum) wins at every income level, ahead by $7,989.70.** The RA is already at the FRS, so neither top-up earns tax relief and the decision is pure interest: the lump sum compounds at 4% for the full 10 years, while Option 2's $8,000 is split each year between the RA and an outside pot earning 2.5%. The result holds for any outside rate below 4.00% (§8).

Full reasoning: year-by-year balances (§5, Tables 4–5), per-bracket net benefit (§7, Table 6), and the two-dimensional decision rule (§8, Tables 7–8, Figure 1).

## 9. Conclusion

**Scenario result:** Existing RA balance is at/above FRS, so **no tax relief attaches to either option**. Option 1 (lump sum) wins at **every income level**, ahead by **$7,989.70**.

**General decision rule (age-55 member, YA 2024+ brackets):**

1. RA at or above FRS (a common position — in 2025, 73.4% of members turning 55 had met the FRS, or the BRS while owning one property, up from 70.5% in 2024; Straits Times, 3 Jul 2026, cited in Table 2): the lump sum wins outright — no relief on either option, and the full amount compounds from Year 1.
2. RA below FRS: annual top-ups only become competitive at high marginal brackets, and only if enough of the 10 years of $8,000 relief stay below FRS. See Table 7 (§8); the rising-FRS caveat below affects this rule in practice.
3. Check MRSS eligibility before choosing: grant-attracting top-ups lose their tax relief (IRAS, from YA 2026). The $1/$1 grant beats the relief it displaces (at most about 24c per dollar at the top rate); MRSS itself targets low incomes (average monthly income at most $4,000, at most one property), so it rarely applies in the income band where Option 2 wins.

**Caveats — simplifications and the direction of each bias.** The model is deliberately simple; the biases below are stated, not quantified, and none changes the broad directions:

- **Time value of tax savings:** savings enter at face value, whether they land in Year 1 or Year 10 (no reinvestment, no discounting). The Year-1 relief is equal in both options and cancels; Option 2's *additional* relief arrives in Years 2–10 and would compound to age 65 if reinvested, so time value favors Option 2, if anything — break-evens in Table 7 are, if anything, too high (by the order of a few thousand dollars of income). Neutral for the at-FRS scenario, where neither option earns any relief.
- **Outside returns pre-tax:** Option 2's outside pot earns 2.5% with no tax on the interest, while CPF interest is tax-free. At a 24% marginal rate a 2.5% outside instrument nets only about 1.9%, so the 4.00% crossover in Table 8 is optimistic for high-income members — the lump sum is more robust than the table suggests.
- **Relief FRS frozen at $220,400:** the relief-applicable FRS is the FRS in force for the YA of the top-up and rises every January (2025: $213,000 → 2026: $220,400 → 2027: $228,200, about 3.5%/year). That rise roughly offsets the headroom the $8,000/year top-ups consume, so on the current trajectory most or all of Option 2's top-ups stay relief-eligible where the model counts fewer years — e.g. with $48,000 of initial headroom the model gives 6 relief years; the rising FRS suggests 10. This bias, which favors Option 2, is the largest of the three: the break-evens for $48,000–$72,000 of headroom are, if anything, too high (likely approaching the $87,283 of the $80,000 row, pending the government's future FRS announcements).
- **Brackets and income constant** across the decade; the outside rate of 2.5% is an assumption, not a guarantee, while the CPF 4% base is a floor; the $80,000 total personal tax-relief cap is ignored.
- **Timing:** a top-up made in calendar year k attracts relief in YA k+1; the model credits the saving in the deposit year. No effect on the nominal totals.
- **Horizon stops at age 65:** from 65 the RA converts to CPF LIFE payouts while the outside pot stays liquid and keeps compounding; the comparison values the two as equal at that point. Liquidity preferences, and the irreversibility of top-ups, are not monetised.

**Net direction:** the at-FRS headline is untouched by the relief-related biases and strengthened by the pre-tax-outside one — the lump sum is at least as robust as shown. Below FRS, two of the three quantitative biases favor Option 2 (including the largest, the rising FRS) and one favors Option 1, so the reported break-evens are, if anything, too high: Option 2's winning region is, if anything, larger than shown.
